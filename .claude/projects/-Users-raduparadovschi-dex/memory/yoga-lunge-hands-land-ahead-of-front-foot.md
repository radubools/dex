Photos of lizard pose read as "front foot next to the hands", but the skeleton
cannot do it. A deeply folded front leg puts the ankle only ~0.35 m forward of
the hip, while the trunk's forward run plus upper arm, forearm and hand put the
fingertips ~0.75 m forward. So the front foot sits beside the *elbow*, with the
hands 0.3–0.45 m ahead of the toes. Don't chase the photo.

**Why:** opening the front knee doesn't fix it — from 93° to 103° the ankle
moves +0.03 m forward but +0.07 m sideways, so a shallower bend only widens the
stance and costs the deep knee the pose is named for.

**How to apply:** get the lateral relation right instead — the foot outside the
same-side hand's line by ~0.25 m, the forearm planted inside the foot — and let
the fore-aft offset fall where the bone lengths put it. Raising the hips shortens
the trunk's forward run (a higher hip means a steeper torso), which is the only
lever that moves the hands back toward the foot. See
[[yoga-hip-is-not-a-skeleton-hinge]] for reading the hip angles back.
